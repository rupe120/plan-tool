  - Fix: Define an explicit ledger and review-record selection rule, bind the selected path and record identity into the envelope, and refuse zero or multiple eligible matches.

`code-reconcile`, `code-review`, and `pull-request-review` require complete current evidence, passing required checks, and zero current open material findings.

`handoffs/README.md` only asks authors to list checks run, so an implementation can treat an empty or partial list as passing and return `converged` without the intended verification.

- HIGH `[disposition-source-coverage]` [confidence: limited]: The shared current/open/material contract applies to every adapter, but steps 5 (`Build plan-audit evidence with exact detail coverage, current code grounding, and a compatible three-snapshot trajectory`) and 6 (`Build code-reconcile evidence from a full current plan-canonical sweep`) have no authoritative disposition source or compatibility rule. Step 4 supplies a review ledger for steps 7 and 8, while the `plan-audit` Input row merely names “current dispositions” and the `code-reconcile` row names none; at HEAD, `review_persistence.Annotation` uses `applied`, `skip`, `failed`, and `open`, not the new `fixed` and `not-applicable` states.

  Architecture:

  ```
  It is `open` unless a current evidence-bound `fixed` or `not-applicable` disposition closes it; `deferred`, `accepted-risk`, and any unsupported disposition remain open. Missing identity, severity, provenance, or disposition facts refuse instead of being inferred. Every adapter carries that same classification, although it owns its evidence requirements.
  ```

  Plan-audit and code-reconcile can therefore either invent a closure mapping or refuse ordinary current evidence.
  - Plan-altitude restatement (delivered evidence, not a verdict on this finding): Every adapter must obtain evidence-bound dispositions before it can classify findings as open or closed.
  - Fix: Define the disposition source and fail-closed mapping for plan-audit and code-reconcile alongside the review-ledger mapping, and require each adapter to refuse when its required disposition evidence is unavailable.
- HIGH `[outlook-authority-boundary]` [confidence: limited]: `redesign-or-rescope` depends on whether a remedy exceeds authority, but Architecture gives the judge only stated intent and settled decisions; steps 2 and 9 never define or supply the authority boundary. This conflicts with Scope guardrails’ rule that the evaluator does not select scope: “authority” can reasonably mean the evaluator’s own authority, which is always limited, or the plan’s allowed scope, which yields a different outlook.

  Architecture — outlook precedence:

  ```
  The substantive outlooks are selected in precedence order: `redesign-or-rescope` when the premise fails, a material structural finding recurs without narrowing, or the remedy exceeds authority; `finish-current-fix` when the remaining material work is the bounded tail of the last accepted correction; `continue` when material in-scope findings remain but are narrowing or becoming less severe; and `converged` only under the context rule above.
  ```

  Architecture — judge input:

  ```
  The judge receives the stated intent, settled decisions, qualified completed snapshots, stable finding identities and severities, dispositions, intervening changes, tests, and current subject identity; it checks premise validity before deciding how findings should close.
  ```
  - Plan-altitude restatement (delivered evidence, not a verdict on this finding): A `redesign-or-rescope` outlook must compare a remedy against the plan’s stated authority boundary.
  - Fix: Include the applicable scope and authority boundary in the relevant evidence envelope and judge context, and test that remedies beyond it produce `redesign-or-rescope` rather than an ordinary correction outlook.
