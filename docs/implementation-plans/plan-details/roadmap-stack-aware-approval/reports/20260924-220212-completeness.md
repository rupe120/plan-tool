- MEDIUM `[stack-route-hop-range]` [confidence: limited]: Step 1 (`Add one stack-route derivation beside source_ancestry_to_root ...`) and step 4 (`Build the whole-stack view, its digest, and its one shared printout`) never say whether the recorded root is itself a hop, and the code makes the wrong reading easy. The Architecture "Stack route" sentence says "any hop from the candidate's recorded root to the candidate". `source_ancestry_to_root` returns the ids "through the root, inclusive", and step 1 sits "beside" it. The root is not always a candidate whose route can be read. It can be an enrolled root (`load_stored_candidate_proposal` raises for a non-candidate version). It can be a previously promoted structural candidate (its stored route is STRUCTURAL). It can be a head published by decision settlement (`roadmap_decision_settlement_enrolled.py` creates it with `create_candidate_version` and stores no candidate evidence). Counting the root as a hop has two effects:
  - A candidate rooted at an enrolled root or a settlement head becomes underivable, because the plan says a hop that "is not a stored candidate" is underivable. Every guard then refuses it.
  - After any structural promotion, every later presentation-only stack reads as structural. It is forced through `--decide-candidate` and refused on the answered route.

  Testing and the acceptance criterion "Legacy candidates without a route, and all-presentation stacks, keep their current routes" do not pin a stack rooted at a promoted structural candidate or a settlement head, so the green suite would not catch this. Sites: Architecture "Stack route"; step 1; step 4 (the hop list and the base of the cumulative diff); Testing; the acceptance criteria listed above; the Risks entry on the derivation being shared.
  - Fix: Define the hops as the unpromoted candidates strictly above the recorded root, so the root's own stored route (or lack of one) is never read and never loaded as a hop. Add the three root kinds (enrolled root, previously promoted structural candidate, settlement-published head) as asserted carve-outs in Testing and the acceptance criteria.

Step 2 makes the entry guard stack-aware and step 3 does the same for the seal loader. An implementer can therefore read a rootless candidate two ways:
  - As underivable, which makes selector-era review and promotion refuse. That contradicts the acceptance criterion "Legacy candidates without a route, and all-presentation stacks, keep their current routes".
  - As having no structural hop, which keeps today's behavior.

  The underivable rule does not settle it, because it covers a hop that "is missing, is not a stored candidate, or fails validation", and a rootless candidate has no hop to fail. Testing names "legacy candidates without a route" but not "without a recorded root". Sites: Architecture "Stack route" and "Guards"; step 1; step 2 (answered-route entry); step 3 (`_load_complete_sealed_candidate`); Testing carve-outs; the acceptance criterion quoted above.
  - Fix: State that a candidate whose evidence records no root has no unpromoted stack to derive. It yields "no structural hop", which is distinct from underivable, so the answered-route entry, the answered-seal loader, and the recovery predicate keep their current behavior for selector-era candidates. Assert that carve-out in Testing and the acceptance criteria alongside the existing legacy-route carve-out.
